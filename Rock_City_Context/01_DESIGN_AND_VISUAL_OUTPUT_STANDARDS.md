# Tech Rock — Design Brief and Preferred Visual Output Style

## Governing rule
**Every Tech Rock visual artifact inherits Rock City Church's visual identity.**

This applies to PDFs, reports, proposals, presentations, diagrams, dashboards, posters, social graphics, internal guides, handover packs, websites and designed technical documentation.

Tech Rock may have a more technical expression, but it must read as a **Rock City sub-brand**, not an unrelated software company.

## Core identity
Church lockup: **ROCK | CITY | CHURCH**

Display typography: **Bebas Neue** where appropriate. Body typography: clean, highly readable sans-serif.

Five-pillar colours:
- Evangelism — #D63D3D
- Worship — #E8A93B
- Discipleship — #3093BD
- Fellowship — #3FA876
- Ministry — #7B4DA0

Use colour intentionally. Do not force all five colours into every composition.

## Preferred visual character
Prefer contemporary editorial composition, deliberate hierarchy, strong typography, controlled colour, spatial depth, layered organic forms where relevant, sophisticated texture, negative space, purposeful imagery and clean information architecture.

Transformation-era caterpillar/cocoon/butterfly motifs may be used when genuinely relevant. Do not paste them onto unrelated technical material merely to make it look branded.

## Tech Rock expression
Technical character should come from the **organisation of information**, not technology clichés.

Appropriate devices include structured grids, modular editorial layouts, system maps, data diagrams, restrained technical annotation, strong typographic hierarchy and interface-inspired organisation where useful.

Avoid generic circuit boards, glowing blue networks, code-rain, gears, decorative cloud icons, random laptops, cyberpunk styling, generic SaaS templates and corporate stock-tech aesthetics.

Do not use sound desks, microphones or speakers as shorthand for Tech Rock.

## Hard exclusions
- No sepia haze.
- No generic church crests or shields.
- No unnecessary icons.
- No invented slogans or decorative filler copy.
- No cheap clip-art or obvious stock-template styling.
- No bent or distorted AI typography.
- Do not add wording merely to fill empty space.

## PDFs, reports and operational documents
Internal documents must still feel intentionally Rock City.

Use clear cover/title hierarchy, readable body copy, consistent sections, useful tables and diagrams, Rock City typography, intentional colour accents, deliberate headers/footers where useful and enough whitespace for scanning.

Do not make reports elaborate at the expense of usability. Do not fall back to generic Word-document styling merely because the artifact is internal.

For black-and-white or ink-conscious printing, preserve hierarchy through typography, spacing, rules and layout rather than depending on colour.

## Presentations
Use the requested aspect ratio. For projection in the Rock City Dome, favour high legibility, large type, sufficient contrast, disciplined information density and imagery that projects clearly. Avoid unnecessarily dark or muddy imagery where a lighter treatment is needed.

A technical deck should visibly belong to the same organisation as Rock City's ministry and campaign work.

## Dashboards and diagrams
Clarity comes first. Rock City typography and colour may structure categories, hierarchy, status and relationships, but branding must never obscure data.

Use icons only when they materially improve comprehension.

## Websites and interfaces
Tech Rock web products can use a systems-oriented or maximalist technology-inspired language when the project brief calls for it, but Rock City identity and usability remain the foundation.

Responsive behaviour, clear hierarchy, readable states, accessible interactions and visible error/connection states matter more than decorative effects.

Do not interpret "tech-inspired" as permission for generic futuristic UI.

## Photography and supplied imagery
Preserve supplied people's likeness. Do not regenerate, beautify, reinterpret or replace a person when fidelity is required.

Use the actual Rock City Dome and supplied environmental references when a project depends on the real venue or neighbourhood.

## Revision discipline
If asked to change one element, preserve approved content and design outside that requested change.

## Quality test
Before delivering a Tech Rock artifact, verify:
- Does this visibly belong to Rock City Church?
- Does it communicate Tech Rock without generic IT clichés?
- Is it usable at its intended print/viewing size?
- Does it preserve the exact project brief and approved elements?
- Is typography legible and correctly spelled?
- Have supplied people and real environments been treated faithfully?
- Is technical information clearer because of the design rather than buried by it?
